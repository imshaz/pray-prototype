Praise report

Answered prayer and community indicators, side by side — ready for the website, socials, and the guide.

What this report is careful never to say. "Crime is down and we prayed" is testimony. "Crime is down because we prayed", printed beside a chart on a funding application, is a claim someone must defend. This report is built to make the honest version easy and the overclaiming version hard — prayer and indicators are shown alongside one another, never one as the cause of the other (PRR-10).

Pray Charleston · Q1 2026 · Jan–Mar
Q1 Praise Report

This quarter, 47 churches and 1,284 pray-ers across the tri-county region gathered nine times to pray for our neighborhoods, our young people, and one another. Below is what we have seen answered, and — separately, and honestly — how a few community measures have moved over the same season.

✦ How we prayed together

47 churches involved  ·  1,284 pray-ers active  ·  9 gatherings held  ·  22 of 38 requests marked answered.

Answered prayer, shared with permission

— A family in West Ashley facing eviction found stable housing after their request went out to the region.
— In North Charleston, a father out of work for months started a new job this quarter.

Shared with permission and without any contact details. Private requests are not included.

Community measures this season

Presented for context, each with its source:

— Reported violent incidents, North Charleston: 312, down 11% vs the prior quarter.
Source: SC Incident-Based Reporting (SLED), Feb 2026

— Unsheltered count, tri-county: 641, down 3% vs the prior count.
Source: Lowcountry Continuum of Care PIT count, Jan 2026

— Youth disconnection rate, ages 16–24: 9.8%, roughly steady.
Source: U.S. Census ACS 5-yr, 2022

We present our prayers and these community measures side by side. We do not claim that one caused the other — we give thanks for what we have seen, and we keep watching, together.
